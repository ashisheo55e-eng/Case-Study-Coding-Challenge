**Five Diverse Case Studies with ICD-10 and CPT codes, plus rationale.**

**Case 1:Cardiology (Hypertension & Routine Electrocardiogram)**

**Patient Scenario**: A 58-year-old established male patient presents for a routine follow-up of essential hypertension. He reports feeling well with no chest pain or shortness of breath. Blood pressure is controlled at 130/80 mmHg. The physician performs a routine resting 12-lead ECG with interpretation and report during the visit.

1.ICD-10 Code: I10 (Essential [primary] hypertension)

2.CPT Code: 93000 (Electrocardiogram, routine ECG with at least 12 leads; with interpretation and report)

3.Coding Rationale: Code I10 accurately captures the chronic condition being managed. Code 93000 represents the comprehensive technical and professional component of the ECG performed during the encounter.

**Case 2: Orthopedics (Acute Right Knee Pain)**

Patient Scenario: A 34-year-old female presents to the outpatient clinic complaining of acute right knee pain after twisting it while playing recreational soccer three days ago. Physical examination reveals mild joint effusion and localized tenderness along the joint line. The provider orders a 2-view diagnostic X-ray of the right knee, which shows no acute fracture.

1.ICD-10 Code: M23.22 (Derangement of meniscus due to old tear or injury, right knee) or S83.511A (Sprain of anterior cruciate ligament of right knee, initial encounter) — Let's use: M25.561 (Pain in right knee) for initial nonspecific presentation if definitive tear isn't confirmed yet, or S83.91XA (Sprain of unspecified site of right knee, initial encounter, initial encounter). Let's go with M25.561.

2.CPT Code: 73560 (Radiologic examination, knee; 1 or 2 views)

3.Coding Rationale: M25.561 reflects the primary symptom (right knee pain) per outpatient coding guidelines when a definitive diagnosis is unconfirmed. CPT 73560 accurately codes the 2-view knee X-ray imaging service.

**Case 3: Dermatology (Suspicious Skin Lesion Excision)**

Patient Scenario: A 45-year-old patient presents with a growing, darkly pigmented lesion on their upper back. The dermatologist performs an excision of the lesion. The excised diameter is 1.2 cm, and the wound is closed with simple layered closure. Pathology later confirms a benign nevus.

1.ICD-10 Code: D22.5 (Melanocytic nevi of trunk)

2.CPT Code: 11402 (Excision, benign lesion including margins, except skin tag, trunk, arms, or legs; excised diameter 1.1 to 2.0 cm)

3.Coding Rationale: D22.5 specifies the benign nature and anatomical location of the lesion. CPT 11402 is selected based on the precise lesion size (1.2 cm falls into the 1.1–2.0 cm bracket) and location on the trunk.

**Case 4: Pulmonology (Acute Bronchitis)**

Patient Scenario: A 42-year-old non-smoker presents with a persistent cough productive of yellowish sputum, low-grade fever, and mild wheezing for the past 5 days. Chest auscultation reveals scattered rhonchi. The physician diagnoses acute bronchitis and prescribes a cough suppressant and bronchodilator inhaler.

1.ICD-10 Code: J20.9 (Acute bronchitis, unspecified)

2.CPT Code: 99213 (Office or other outpatient visit for the evaluation and management of an established patient, low severity/complexity)

3.Coding Rationale: J20.9 maps directly to the acute lower respiratory infection diagnosed. An E/M code (99213) is assigned for the straightforward clinical evaluation, history taking, and medical decision-making.

**Case 5: Gastroenterology (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease - GERD)**

Patient Scenario: A 50-year-old patient visits the clinic reporting frequent burning retrosternal chest discomfort, particularly after meals and when lying flat, occurring 3–4 times a week over the last two months. The physician diagnoses GERD without esophagitis and recommends lifestyle modifications alongside a trial of proton pump inhibitors.

1.ICD-10 Code: K21.9 (Gastro-esophageal reflux disease without esophagitis)

2.CPT Code: 99212 (Office or other outpatient visit, established patient, straightforward medical decision-making)

3.Coding Rationale: K21.9 captures the specific digestive disorder diagnosed without complications. CPT 99212 accounts for the straightforward office evaluation and management service.
